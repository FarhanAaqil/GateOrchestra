`results/real/analysis/per_task_outcomes.json` (extracted from the final benchmark's checkpoints).

1/3, 2/3 or 1, so only three threshold gates exist. Each is scored on the recorded outcomes of the

## What it means

On this benchmark the learned gate is not measurably better than the one-line rule: 97.9% at 784 tokens against 96.9% at 722 for `never` (p = 0.5). The ceiling is low for any gate: an oracle that escalates only when Always-MAS fixes a wrong probe answer reaches 99.0% by escalating 2.1% of runs, against 96.9% for never escalating. Escalating only when all three samples disagree (t=2/3) reaches 97.4% at 736 tokens. The simulated gate (780 tokens) tracks the live one (784), which supports the scoring. The gate's measured value here is that it learned not to escalate; a benchmark where the multi-agent system wins more often is needed to show a learned gate earning its keep over a threshold.
